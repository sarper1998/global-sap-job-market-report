Candidate Interpretation

Prioritize the role family that appears across evidence layers.

Both detailed evidence layers point to Technical / Development as the leading role family: 225 of 631 open-feed postings (35.7%) and 1,162 of 2,034 direct employer jobs (57.1%). In the LinkedIn collected pool, the largest classified role after broad "Other SAP-Related" is Functional Consulting with 17,912 links (16.1%).

Pick a module lane instead of staying generic.

Open-feed module mentions are led by S/4HANA 116 (18.4%), HCM / SuccessFactors 80 (12.7%), BTP / Integration 56 (8.9%), FI / CO / FICO 45 (7.1%). Direct employer postings currently skew toward HCM / SuccessFactors 891 (43.8%), S/4HANA 480 (23.6%), BTP / Integration 349 (17.2%), HANA / Data 261 (12.8%). In LinkedIn records where an identifiable SAP area is present, the leading signals are S/4HANA 6,034 (5.4%), FI / CO / FICO 5,057 (4.5%), ABAP / Development 3,542 (3.2%), BTP / Integration 3,320 (3.0%). The clearest cross-source overlap is S/4HANA, HCM / SuccessFactors, BTP / Integration. That makes those areas stronger candidate bets than a source-specific spike alone.

Build a portfolio around current technical terms.

The strongest open-feed technical mentions are S/4HANA 116 (18.4%), Agile / Scrum 66 (10.5%), SuccessFactors 57 (9.0%), SAP BTP 39 (6.2%). Direct employer postings emphasize SuccessFactors 865 (42.5%), S/4HANA 480 (23.6%), SAP BTP 290 (14.3%), Agile / Scrum 286 (14.1%), while the LinkedIn collected pool shows S/4HANA 6,034 (5.4%), FICO 3,720 (3.3%), ABAP 3,218 (2.9%), SAP HANA 2,377 (2.1%). A practical candidate portfolio should show one core module plus integration, data, migration, or implementation evidence.

Soft skills are a market signal, not decoration.

Open-feed descriptions mention Consulting mindset 125 (19.8%), Leadership 77 (12.2%), Change management 58 (9.2%), Analytical thinking 30 (4.8%). Direct employer pages add Consulting mindset 326 (16.0%), Change management 209 (10.3%), Leadership 102 (5.0%), Analytical thinking 45 (2.2%), and the LinkedIn pool surfaces Consulting mindset 24,790 (22.3%), Leadership 11,249 (10.1%), Communication 9,298 (8.4%), Analytical thinking 7,738 (7.0%). For SAP roles, stakeholder work, rollout/change experience, documentation, training, and cross-functional communication should be visible in CVs and interviews.

Degrees point to useful entry routes, but skills drive the screen.

Open-feed education-field signals are Computer Science / Informatics 154 (24.4%), Engineering 143 (22.7%), Finance / Accounting 80 (12.7%), Logistics / Supply Chain 41 (6.5%). Direct employer pages show Computer Science / Informatics 974 (47.9%), Engineering 359 (17.6%), Finance / Accounting 97 (4.8%), Logistics / Supply Chain 80 (3.9%), and LinkedIn collected records show Computer Science / Informatics 15,901 (14.3%), Engineering 12,740 (11.4%), Finance / Accounting 9,712 (8.7%), Logistics / Supply Chain 9,213 (8.3%). The pattern favors computer science/informatics, engineering, finance/accounting, and logistics/supply-chain routes, depending on the SAP module path.

Salary research needs a separate phase.

Salary visibility is partial: 27.4% of the open-feed sample includes pay information (173 of 631 postings). Salary cuts by module and country are useful, but still need caveats.

Early-Career Direction

The current data suggests choosing one of several concrete entry lanes rather than trying to be "an SAP person" in general.

  • Technical path: start with S/4HANA, ABAP, BTP, HANA/data, or Fiori/UI5 if your background is computer science, informatics, or engineering.
  • Functional path: use finance/accounting, logistics/supply-chain, HR, procurement, sales, or manufacturing knowledge to enter FI/CO, MM, SD, EWM, PP/QM, or SuccessFactors roles.
  • Consulting path: combine a module with stakeholder workshops, documentation, testing, migration, training, and change-management examples.
  • Signal gap: The LinkedIn pool is link-rich but still description-light: 20,971 rows have fetched description detail (18.8% of the collected LinkedIn pool). Skill and soft-skill signals from LinkedIn should be read as early directional evidence.

Why LinkedIn Has Overlap

LinkedIn search results are not a clean one-row-per-job database. The same posting can appear under several searches before deduplication, and some near-duplicate postings are genuinely separate location or employer variants.

  • The same posting can appear across multiple keywords such as SAP, SAP SD, S/4HANA, FICO, ABAP, and BTP.
  • The same posting can appear across location searches when a role is advertised for multiple cities, countries, or regions.
  • The same posting can appear across filters such as all jobs, remote, hybrid, past week, and past month.
  • Some companies publish one role as many location-specific postings; some staffing and aggregator listings describe the same underlying opening more than once.
  • For analysis, the report deduplicates mainly by LinkedIn job id and treats the remaining 111,315 records as the evidence pool, while 371,000+ remains only the market-size signal.

Methodology

  • Open job data was collected from public job-board feeds and normalized into one common structure: title, company, location, salary fields, source link, SAP focus, role family, seniority, module, and skills.
  • Deduplication removes repeated or near-identical postings by comparing normalized title, company, location, and source URL signals, so the same job is less likely to be counted multiple times across feeds.
  • SAP matching used strong keyword signals such as SAP, S/4HANA, ABAP, SuccessFactors, Ariba, Fiori, UI5, BTP, HANA, BW/4HANA, and related terms.
  • Salary was not estimated. A posting was marked as salary-disclosed only when the source provided salary fields or the description explicitly mentioned compensation.
  • LinkedIn has two separate layers: rounded LinkedIn Jobs search-result counts, and a collected public guest job-link pool gathered through partitioned searches.
  • LinkedIn result overlap is expected. The same posting may appear across multiple keyword, location, work-model, and recency searches; deduplication mainly uses LinkedIn job id to avoid counting those repeated appearances as separate evidence records.
  • Company career data is collected from public career pages and ATS endpoints such as jobs2web / SuccessFactors, SmartRecruiters, Greenhouse, and Workday CXS. These runs are parallel to the LinkedIn crawler and stored as a separate source-linked layer.
  • The LinkedIn guest scraper does not use logged-in cookies, proxy rotation, CAPTCHA bypass, or private profile/session data. The 371,000+ figure is treated as market size; the collected guest pool is the source-linked subset available for analysis and download.
